Buys

  • one contract → many models; fallback lists and retry across providers
  • routing by price / latency / throughput percentile + A-B
  • budgets, cost analytics, key custody, audit log
  • a place to pin alias → model@version so nothing swaps silently

Never buys

  • Compliance does not transfer. Retention and geo terms come from the model vendor underneath
  • fees: OpenRouter 5.5% on credits ($0.80 min), 5% BYOK; Portkey Free 10k logs → Pro $49/mo → $9/100k requests
  • a self-hosted gateway adds ops + a hop of latency
  • quality: routing cannot make a weaker model answer your hardest 5%

When it wins

  • Experimentation: try 30 models this week, no legal review each
  • Bursty or low volume: 5.5% of a small bill < your procurement salary
  • Fallback plumbing you'd otherwise write badly

When it loses

  • Scale: 5.5% on a $120k/mo bill is $6.6k/mo for a routing layer
  • Regulated data: you now have a fourth party in the path and no negotiated DPA with the model vendor
  • You already have EA/VCRC discounts a list-price router won't pass through

Why a European should care

  • It prices sovereignty of access as a product feature
  • "No fee stacking" is a real pricing claim: you pay the pool's top-tier rate, once

You move from a direct Anthropic contract to OpenRouter for convenience. What did not change?
A gateway adds a party and changes pricing; it does not change the model vendor's retention or training terms — those follow the model underneath. If your DPA says "no retention", you must verify the terms of the actual provider that served each request.

Leaving

The contract you should sign is the one you can walk out of in a fortnight.

the lock-in inventory

What actually pins you is never the API shape

Pins (ranked by how much they hurt)

  • Embeddings — different dimensions ⇒ re-embed the whole corpus
  • Fine-tuned weights that never leave the vendor's platform
  • Committed capacity — Azure PTU $312/PTU/mo, AWS/GCP commit SKUs
  • Retention regimes — Fable/Mythos Covered Models: 30-day retention, no ZDR [AN-R]
  • Prompt/eval suites tuned to one vendor's quirks & tool dialects
  • Tokener drift — 4.7+ ≈ +30% tokens for identical text [AN-P]

Portable (make these the majority)

  • OpenAI-compatible endpoints — Azure APIM speaks Chat/Responses and Anthropic Messages [APIM]; Kimi, Sakana, Mistral all expose them
  • Open weights: Mistral Large 3 (Apache-2.0), gpt-oss, Qwen, GLM → NIM / Dynamo / vLLM
  • Your own eval harness, golden sets and red-team suite — vendor-neutral by construction
  • Batch artifacts, cached prompts, and the tool schemas you wrote
Rule of thumb: if switching costs more than two quarters of the bill differential, you don't have a vendor — you have a dependency. Price that dependency at the start of the negotiation, not at the renewal.

Your vendor announces a 40% price rise effective in 60 days on a model serving 70% of your traffic. What is the first move?
In 60 days you cannot qualify a vendor: legal, security, eval and integration each take longer than that. The only fast lever is the source you already approved — which is exactly why the second source is a standing capability, not a project triggered by bad news.

recap

Five things to remember

  • The unit of procurement is a platform contract, not a model: processing region, retention, throughput reservation, exit cost.
  • The spread is 33× inside one cloud — $0.15 to $5 per 1M input on Bedrock/Foundry alone. Cascade routing is where that money lives.
  • Discounts are arithmetic, premiums are policy: batch ≈−50%, cache hit ≈−90% of input, priority +75–80% (Azure PP 2.5×), geo +10%, and they often don't stack.
  • Gateways buy speed and failover; they never transfer compliance. OpenRouter = marketplace for 5.5%; Fugu = orchestrator selling access sovereignty (and not in the EU/EEA yet).
