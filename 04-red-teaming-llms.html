the stakes

Safety alignment is a thin, English-heavy veneer

79%
GPT-4 harmful-compliance rate when unsafe English prompts are translated to low-resource languages — vs ~0% in English (Yong et al., 2023)
80.9%
unsafe output from multilingual jailbreak templates on ChatGPT (MultiJail, ICLR 2024)
~50%
malicious agent tasks leading models comply with — unjailed, in AgentHarm (ICLR 2025)
>30%
some SOTA models' false-refusal rate on clearly-safe XSTest prompts — the other failure mode
Two ways to fail: answering what you shouldn't (harm) and refusing what you should (over-refusal). A red team measures both. ASR without both sides is marketing.
Sources: arXiv:2310.02446 (NeurIPS SoLaR best paper), arXiv:2310.06474, arXiv:2410.09024, arXiv:2308.01263. Note "compliance" ≠ "correct instructions" — scored by classifier/judge with StrongREJECT-style rubric.

threat model

What are you actually attacking?

  • Raw model → jailbreaks, harmful content, prompt leakage.
  • RAG / knowledge app → indirect injection through documents, sensitive-data disclosure, poisoning.
  • Agent with tools → excessive agency: injected email says "forward secrets to X", the agent has an email tool. This is where red-teaming matters most in 2026.
  • Map each finding to OWASP LLM Top10: LLM01 injection, LLM02 data leak, LLM06 excessive agency, LLM07 prompt leak, LLM10 DoS…

Red-team question #1: can untrusted text make the box below it act?

Classic pentest discipline: scope + trust boundaries. LLMs make every piece of read text a potential instruction — "prompt injection is the new SQL injection" (but unfixable by parameterization; mitigation is architecture: least privilege, isolation, human-in-loop for irreversible actions).

scoring that doesn't lie

ASR: the number, and its three lies

  • ASR = harmful responses ÷ attempts — scored by judge classifier (WildGuard, Llama Guard 3) or LLM-judge rubric.
  • Lie 1 — empty jailbreak: model rambles without content, keyword-matcher counts "success". StrongREJECT's rubric (specific + useful harm) exposes it: many "95%" jailbreaks → near 0.
  • Lie 2 — single sample: refusals are stochastic across seeds/temperature; report ASR over ≥3–5 samples.
  • Lie 3 — no false-positive side: pair every harm suite with XSTest — refusing everything is also failure.

Example launch gates

  • ASR < 5% on HarmBench-standard (StrongREJECT-scored)
  • XSTest over-refusal < 5%
  • 0 tool-actions driven by injected content (AgentDojo suite)
  • 0 PII in outputs; system prompt extraction = 0
  • all findings: severity + repro + remediation + retest
"Expected outcome" from the brief, formalized: pass/fail gates + the report. Mention: refusal instability paper (arXiv 2512.12066) — same prompt flips refusals across seeds; single-shot safety evals are fiction.

The multilingual gap

The most actionable, least-glamorous finding in LLM safety.

≈14 min in. This is the differentiating section of the talk.
the evidence

Same attack, different language, opposite result

Study (year)English ASRHigh-resourceLow-resource
Yong et al. — translate-only attacks, GPT-3.5/4 (2023)~3% / ~0%30–60%79–95%+ (Zulu, Xhosa, Lao, Somali)
MultiJail — 8-language templates (ICLR 2024)baseline2–3×80.9% ChatGPT · 40.7% GPT-4
Chinese/Russian jailbreaks (2023-24)blockedpartially workszh/ru templates bypass while EN fails
Newest multilingual models (2025-26)gap narrows with multilingual safety datanever closes — small open models worst
Why (1): safety training (RLHF/DPO pairs) is overwhelmingly English. Alignment doesn't transfer for free.
Why (2): tokenization — low-resource scripts fragment into rare tokens (talk 03!). Safety behavior is per-token under-trained exactly where tokens are rarest.
arXiv:2310.02446; arXiv:2310.06474; CValues (2307.09717) & HarmfulQA (2402.14887) are the rare non-EN safety sets.
The tokenization↔safety link is the deck-series crossover: rare tokens = under-trained everything, including refusal. Free translation APIs mean any attacker has a multilingual pipeline — the paper's core alarm: "what hurt low-resource speakers now threatens all users".

the deliverable

A report that gets fixed

Finding FMT-004 · HIGH

  • Category: LLM01 indirect injection → LLM02 disclosure
  • Repro: full prompt/tool transcript (attached)
  • Impact×likelihood: any user's invoice readable via planted webpage (High×Med)
  • Expected vs actual: agent should ignore doc-instructions; it exfiltrated
  • Remediation: content quarantine + domain-allowlist + confirm-before-send
  • Retest: ✓ blocked after fix (commit link)
  • Exec summary (risk posture, top-3) + full findings appendix + repro dataset
  • ASR heatmap per family×language (your new KPI), over-refusal alongside
  • Every finding: severity, repro, remediation, retest — red-teaming without retest is complaining
Report shape matters — devs fix tickets with repro + severity + suggested fix. Tie remediations to architecture (least privilege) not "prompt harder".
A jailbreak paper reports 98% ASR on GPT-4 via "new template X". Your StrongREJECT-scored rerun gets 6%. Most likely explanation?
Exactly the "empty jailbreak" problem StrongREJECT was built to measure — compliance quality and specificity, not refusal-keyword absence. The field's ASR numbers only mean something with honest scoring.

recap

Five things to remember

  • Method > vibes: threat-model → 5 attack families → honest scoring → severity report → retest.
  • Score both sides: ASR (with StrongREJECT-style rubric, multi-sample) + XSTest over-refusal.
  • The multilingual gap is real and cheap to find: translated suite + low-resource arms; expect 79–95% ASR in the weakest languages where EN is ~0%.
  • Automate: garak (model scan, lat), promptfoo (CI gates), PyRIT (adaptive) — humans for creativity, not volume.
  • Fixes are architectural: guards buy time; least-privilege tools, content quarantine and human-in-loop buy safety.
